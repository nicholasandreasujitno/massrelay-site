Four problems a room fixes.

Each one runs the same loop: ask, propose, approve, work, finish, receipt. The loop is the product; the use case is what you put in it.

A small business and its accountant

The books are late, the receipts are in a drawer, and nobody trusts a number that an app typed in by itself.

Two sides in one room. The agent folds the month; the accountant signs it; the receipt names both, the hash proves it, the cost splits as agreed.

A team paying for five assistants

Everyone has their own subscription, nothing is shared, and the costs are invisible until the card statement.

One room, one shared memory under clearance, one bill. A member's laptop running a local model serves the whole room for free; the receipt shows what each task cost and what the top model would have.

A professional with clients

Finding clients is a marketplace page; getting paid is a chase; proving the work was yours is a screenshot.

A public front room: what you offer, your published receipts, a hire button. Hiring opens a private room with the deliverable pinned; you are paid on verification, and the receipt is your credit.

A record that is yours, not an app's

A calorie tracker, a running log, a baby diary: each one a subscription, each one a silo, each one guessing.

The same room with a typed record. The bot asks instead of guessing, the fold is yours on your own machine, and a verified professional can review it as a paid deliverable.

One room. Everyone's agent. One record.

People sit in the room with their own agents. Every action an agent wants to take becomes a proposal; a person approves; what happens is written to a record nobody can edit, and the receipt is built from it.

The price of a task, shown. Routing is why it is small.

Every answer can show what it cost and what the top model would have cost. Example figures for one monthly-books task; the real numbers are on your receipt.

Hosted by us, or by you.

The kernel is identity, tenancy, policy gates, an append-only audit chain and a ledger. Every consequential write passes a gate and leaves an audit row. Row-level security keeps one workspace from ever reading another's rows.

Rust and TypeScript on Postgres, one Docker Compose, no inbound port. A self-hosted community edition with the same code is planned once the export checks are green. Until then, the hosted service opens early rooms by invitation.
